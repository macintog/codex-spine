# Sources And Provenance

This skill synthesizes public visualization, graphical-perception,
accessibility, and statistical practice. It derives principles; it does not
reproduce protected book pages, proprietary examples, or finished visual
artifacts.

Public links were reviewed for the July 18, 2026 revision. Book links identify
the source works; the skill does not claim that every instruction is a direct
quotation or page-specific paraphrase.

## Graphical Perception

- William S. Cleveland and Robert McGill, "Graphical Perception: Theory,
  Experimentation, and Application to the Development of Graphical Methods,"
  *Journal of the American Statistical Association* 79(387), 1984:
  https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1984.10478080

This work informs the preference for position and length on common scales over
less precisely decoded encodings.

## Accessibility

- W3C, *Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2*:
  https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/
- W3C Web Accessibility Initiative, *Complex Images*:
  https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/images/complex/

These sources inform contrast, non-color encoding, interaction access, motion,
and text equivalents. Medium-specific skills and standards remain authoritative
for their delivery formats.

## Statistical Practice

- NIST/SEMATECH, *e-Handbook of Statistical Methods*:
  https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/

This source supports the skill's insistence that interval type, sample,
transformation, model, missingness, and inferential status be named rather than
collapsed into generic visual uncertainty.
